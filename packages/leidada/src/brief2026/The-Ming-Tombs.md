# The Ming Tombs

Ladies and gentlemen, my friends, I will be your guide today.

And it's my great honor to show you around the Ming Tombs.

We are now 30 minutes away from the Ming Tombs, And I'd like to give you a brief introduction about it.

The Ming Tombs are located in Changping District about 50 kilometers to the northwest of the Beijing.

There are total of 13 Ming Emperors buried in this area, so it is called the 'Thirteen Ming Tombs' in Chinese.

A Fengshui master, called Liao Junqing, was in charge of whole project and finally Chose the current area, a basin encircled by mountains with water floating in the front.

Emperor Zhudi was greatly satisfied with this burial ground and ordered the construction of his tomb. He changed the name of Yellow Earth Hill to Heavenly Longevity Hill.

Ok, ladies and gentlemen, now let's make our way into the Changling.

Changling is located at the foot of Heavenly Longevity Hill. It is the head tomb of the Thirteen Ming Tombs, the largest tomb of them all and it is also the best-preserved one in this tomb area.

The layout of Changling just followed the example of Xiaoling in nanjing. Structures along the central axis are: the Front Gate to the Tomb, the Gate of Eminent Favor, the Hall of Eminent Favor, the Dragon and Phoenix Gate, Soul Tower and the wall-encircled Earth Mound.

The Gate of Eminent Favor and the Hall of Eminent Favor of Changling on the central axis are still standing today. That is why we say that Changling is the best-preserved tomb in the Ming Tombs area.

There is a stone tablet housed in a pavilion on the east side inside the front gate.

Originally, there was no inscription on it. The inscriptions we see today on three sides were written by three emperors:Qing Emperor Shuzhi, written in the Manchu and Han languages, inscribed the order to protect the Ming Tombs; Emperor Qianlong and Emperor Jiaqing inscribed two poems paying homage to the Ming Tombs.

The Hall of Eminent Favor is also called 'Sacrificial Hall' where the sacrificial ceremonies were held by the later emperors for their ancestors.

It is one of the largest wooden buildings in China, and it is supported by 60 wooden columns made of precious Nanmu. Nanmu is a special kind of wood coming from the mountains in the southern  part of our country. This kind of valuable timber comes from Sichuan, Hubei, Hunan and Jiangxi provinces.

Dinglin is the tomb of Emperor Zhu Yijun, whose reign title is Wang Li, the 13th Emperor of the Ming Dynasty.

The construction of his tomb started when Emperor Wang Li was only 22 years old.

When he was 6 years old, he was chosen and named Crown Prince and ascended the throne at the age of 10. He ruled China for 48 years, the longest ruling period of all the 16 emperors in the Ming Dynasty.

We now entering the gate of Dingling, you can see two sketch maps.

Since the Gate of Eminent Faver and the Hall of Eminent Faver were destroyed, only the marble terraces are left today. The Soul Tower  stands and the end of the axis with two exhibition rooms on both sides.

I'd like to give you some information about the excavation of Dingling. It started in May 1956 and it took two years and was completed in 1958.

In 1955, a decision was made by the Chinese government to open up the Ming Tombs. According to the original plan, Changling should be  excavated first. However, because of the lack of experience, a decision was made to open up a smaller one first.

In May 1956,  an archaeological team found some decayed bricks at the southwest side of the surrounding wall outside the Soul Tower showing that there had been a archway. It was believed that the exposed arch was the tomb tunnel to the underground palace. Later, traces of words like, 'the gate of leading tunnel' were found on the wall. This offered a very important clue for the tomb's excavation.

And in the following months, two tunnels were dug consecutively, till a stone tablet was uncovered with an inscription on which written ' from this stone, 160 feet further and 35 deep to the Diamond Wall' which indicated the sealing wall of the underground palace.

So, the archaeologists got a very important clue from this stone for the further excavation of the underground tomb. Then they dug up a 3rd tunnel and finally found the Diamond Wall, the entrance of the underground palace.

The underground palace consists of five chambers: the Front Chamber, the Middle Chamber, the Rear Chamber and annex Chambers on the left and right.

In front of the Front Chamber is the Diamond Wall, the sealing wall to the Underground Tomb.

Among all five chambers, he Middle Chamber and Rear Chamber are important. There are three marble thrones place here for the emperor and his two wives.

And the Rear Chamber is the main one and the largest one in the palace. There are three coffins, The middle one is for the emperor, and his two wives. There are 26 wooden boxes, containing the precious funeral objects. More than 3000 pieces of precious objects were unearthed form.
