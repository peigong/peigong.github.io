# The Summer Palace

Ladies and gentlemen, my friends. I will be your guide today.

And it is my great honor to show you around the Summer Palace.

We are now in front of the east gate of the Summer Palace, and I'd like to give you a brief introduction about  It.

The Summer Palace is located on the northwest of outskirts of Beijing, about 20 kilometers away from the center of the city.

In 1998, it was listed as the World Culture Heritage.

The Summer Palace was first built as an imperial garden and palace in the Jin dynasty, the construction continued to the Yuan and Ming dynasties, and the palace was enlarged in the Qing dynasty.

In the Qing dynasty during the reign of Emperor Qianlong, large-scale construction of imperial gardens reached its peak. And the Garden of Clear Ripples was its origin.

During the reign of Empress Dowager Cixi, the Garden of Clear Ripples was renamed as the Summer Palace.

In 1860, the Anglo-French allied forces invaded Beijing, and three hills and five gardens were burnt down to ashes.

In 1900, the allied forces of Eight Powers invaded Beijing. The Summer Palace was damaged by the invaders again.

In 1924, the Summer Palace was turned into a public park.

Now, let's take a look at the East Palace Gate. The East Palace Gate is the main entrance to the Summer Palace.

While, please take a look at the plaque above the middle gate that bears 3 big Chinese characters 'the Summer Palace' in Emperor Guangxu's handwriting.

Entering the East Palace Gate, we will see the Gate of Benevolence and Longevity. It is the second gate in the palace area.

Now, we have come to the Hall of Benevolence and Longevity. The hall was the place where Emperor Guangxu and Empress Dowager Cixi held the audience and handle the state affairs when they were in the Summer Palace.

In front of the hall stands a bronze animal called Qilin. It is believed that the Qilin was able to distinguish the right from the wrong.

Besides the Qilin, you will also see a pair of bronze incense burners in the shape of dragon and phoenix are placed in front of the Hall of Benevolence and Longevity.

Inside the Hall of Benevolence and Longevity, people can see the emperor's throne carved with a 9-dragons design.

Ok. Let's keep walking with me and I will show you the next place.

The Hall of Jade Ripples was built along the bank of Kunming Lake behind the Hall of Benevolence and Longevity by Qing Emperor Qianlong.

During Emperor Guangxu reign, it was Emperor Guangxu's private living quarters. But after the failure of the Reform Movement, Emperor Guangxu was under house arrest here.

There are two annex chambers, Xiafenshi to the east and Ouxiangxie to the west. The two chambers were sealed by brick walls. The brick walls reached to the ceiling.

And the Yiyunguan, that stays at the very rear was the bedroom for Empress Longyu.

Ok, my friends, let's keep moving with me.

The Hall of Joyful Longevity was the residence for Empress Dowager.

This hall consists of 4 chambers. To the east, the outer-chamber was the breakfast room and tea room for her. The east inner-chamber was her dressing room. The west inner-room was her bedroom and the west outer-room was the room for her to read and sign the documents.

A large table in the central hall served as a dining table for Empress Dowager Cixi. A big porcelain jar on each side was used to contain fruits to produce fragrant smell.

In the courtyard, there is a huge rock named 'Qin Zhi Xiu'. There is an interesting story about it, that an official called Miwanzhong in the Ming Dynasty, who loved this rock so much in Fangshan, and he want to move it to his garden, but unfortunately, he was not able to afford it. Then when Emperor Qianlong discovered it afterwards, he ordered to move this rock to the current place.

Okay, ladies and gentlemen, let's keep moving. We will see the Long Corridor.

The Long Corridor actually is a long covered walkway. It is 728 meters long with 273 sections, rated as the longest, biggest and most famous one in China and even in the world.

It lies in front of Longevity Hill with the Gate of Dispelling Clouds in the center, and four pavilions along the Long Corridor, symbolizing the four seasons of the year.

They are totally over 14000 paintings and pictures, including both large and small paintings and pictures. Of the 14000 paintings, there are more than 8000 large paintings.

Ladies and gentlemen, here is the halfway of the Long Corridor. And this area is called the Gate of Dispelling Clouds.

This gate is located in the center of the Long Corridor, and it is the main entrance to the Hall of Dispelling Clouds.

Entering the main gate, you will see the second gate where Emperor Guangxu kowtowed to Empress Dowager Cixi on her birthday celebration.

In center of the Hall of Dispelling Clouds, is the nine-dragon throne. This was the throne for Empress Dowager Cixi to sit during her birthday celebration.

The Tower of Buddhist Incense, the symbol of the Summer Palace, standing in the center of the southern slope of Longevity Hill, and also the center of the whole imperial garden.

The Temple of the Sea of Wisdom, a stone structure, is located on the top of the hill, just behind the Tower of Buddhist Incense. This building is also known as 'Beamless Hall', because it was built without using a single beam or column in its structure.
